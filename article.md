# What is Phishing and How to Stay Safe Online

Have you ever received a fake email or text message claiming you won a prize, or warning that your bank account will be blocked unless you click a link right away? That is called **phishing**.

Phishing is a common cyber attack where scammers pretend to be a trusted company—like a bank, delivery service, or social media app—to trick you into sharing your passwords, OTPs, or credit card details.

## How a Phishing Attack Works

1. **The Bait:** A scammer sends an urgent email, SMS, or WhatsApp message that looks like it came from a real company.
2. **The Trap:** The message asks you to click a link and log in to fix a fake problem (such as "Your password has expired").
3. **The Theft:** The link opens a fake website that looks identical to the real one. When you type your username and password, the scammer steals them.

## 4 Warning Signs of a Phishing Message

You can spot most phishing attempts by looking for these red flags:

- **Urgent or scary language:** Messages saying "Act within 24 hours or your account will be deleted!" try to make you panic so you do not think carefully.
- **Strange sender addresses:** An email claiming to be from a major bank might come from a weird address like `support@bank-security-update123.com`.
- **Generic greetings:** Real companies usually use your actual name, whereas scammers often write "Dear Customer" or "Dear User."
- **Suspicious links:** Hovering your mouse over a link (or pressing and holding on a phone) often reveals a different web address than the one shown in the text.

## How to Protect Yourself

- **Never click suspicious links:** Instead, open your browser and type the official website address yourself.
- **Turn on Two-Factor Authentication (2FA):** Even if a scammer steals your password, 2FA blocks them from logging in without the extra code sent to your phone.
- **Never share your OTP:** No genuine bank or company employee will ever ask you for a One-Time Password.
